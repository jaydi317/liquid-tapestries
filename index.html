What we make

No two jobs are alike. These are the finishes we’re asked for most.

  • Polished or matte mineral plaster, built up by hand in thin layers until the wall has depth you can see into. Our most-requested finish right now.

  • Onyx, Carrara, Calacatta and black marble, painted where real stone is too heavy, too costly or simply won’t fit.

  • Walnut, oak and weathered barnwood, painted onto doors, trim, cabinets and beams so new work matches old.

  • Safari rooms, beach scenes, old-world skies, a flower-wreath dome, a wall that says BELIEVE. If you can picture it, we can paint it.

  • Gold and silver leaf ceilings, bronze plaster walls, copper and zinc hoods, rusted iron.

  • Brick vaults, crumbling plaster, stone arches: kitchens, cellars, bars and restaurants that feel a hundred years old on day one.

  • Carved concrete walls, wine cellars and grottos for a few projects a year.

Also known as faux finishes, decorative painting and specialty finishes.

Your sample is the standard.

Dave’s rule: “I can’t read your mind.” So we start with yours, and we don’t touch your wall until you’ve approved a sample.

  1. Tell us about the room.

    Send the form or call. Photos, the feel you’re after, your designer’s name if you have one.

  2. Dave comes out.

    He looks at the space, the light, and what’s already there.

  3. We make samples.

    Real samples of your finish, in your colors, at $100 each. confirm: credited to the job?

  4. You approve one.

    If we’re not there yet, we run it again. The approved sample becomes the standard for the whole job.

  5. Written price, then the work.

    The same crew of three, from first coat to final walk-through.

What affects the price

  • Size of the surface
  • How many passes the finish needs
  • The materials
  • Access (ceilings, scaffolding)

No price list, because no two jobs are alike.

He wrote the book. Literally.

Dave Schmidt has been painting walls that fool people since about 1985 confirm. In the ’90s he co-owned Prismatic Painting Studio, a decorative-painting school in Cincinnati, and taught the craft on stage and on video. In 2001 he co-wrote Marvelous Murals You Can Paint with muralist Gary Lord. Today he works with a crew of three on homes and restaurants across the Indianapolis area, mostly for designers and builders who’ve called him for years.
